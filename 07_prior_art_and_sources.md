# 07 — Prior art and source register

[Start](README.md) · [Previous: research plan](06_research_plan_and_evidence.md) · [Next: defensive structural disclosure](08_defensive_structural_disclosure.md)

**21 October 2026.** This register lists systems, papers, standards and patent disclosures relevant to ASKA, including leads that need further review. Entries use IDs A01–G03 or short names and state what was inspected and what the comparison covers.

## What attribution does and does not establish

The seven statements in [Document 08](08_defensive_structural_disclosure.md#prior-art-and-scope) specify three apparatus relationships and dependent conditions. The comparison table identifies established components and the ordering or authority relationship still to assess. The novelty and nonobviousness of the complete arrangements remain unresolved.

A **direct mechanism precedent** supports the identified part. A **partial comparator** differs in fields, ordering, trust or coverage. A **discovery reference** is a lead that still needs checking against the relevant requirements. **Unmapped** means a requirement was not located in the inspected material; its originality remains unknown. A dependent statement includes its parent’s requirements. Assess the whole statement even when its added mechanism is known. Formal legal criteria are separate: [MPEP2131](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2131.html), [MPEP2141](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2141.html).

Review depth varies by entry. “Full relevant sections” means the listed passages were read; “discovery depth” means abstracts, records or excerpts. The review ran no artifacts and produced no ASKA hardware, proof or performance results. Preprints, individual drafts, vendor specifications and patent descriptions retain those statuses. A patent’s publication date here is not a determination of priority entitlement, validity or freedom to operate.

## Maintaining the comparisons

Before asserting a distinction, read the relevant passages and record the source’s trusted components, policy semantics and ordering. Identify any ASKA requirements still unmatched. Check abstract-only leads against the full text. When combining sources, explain why their mechanisms would be combined and whether their semantics and trust assumptions are compatible.

The diagrams, hypothetical traces, field widths, capacities and calculations are ASKA design examples. Citations credit the mechanisms and comparison systems, while the numerical choices belong to these examples. Measured results must identify the implementation revision, apparatus and commands needed to reproduce them.

## Short glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| IES | Isolated Execution Stack: proposed hardware-enforced computation and resource compartment |
| Gate | Mandatory enforcement function at a defined boundary; implementation may include protected firmware |
| Realm | An independently administered set of resources and policy authorities |
| Capability | Protected authority to perform specified operations on an object; not automatically a data-flow label |
| Label | Protected information-flow restriction interpreted under a defined policy |
| Release | Explicit authorization for a particular otherwise-forbidden disclosure |
| Session | Installed directional authority, interpretation, cryptographic, and freshness state |
| Boundary continuity | The proposed preservation of authority and restrictions across representation and enforcement boundaries |
| Protected state | State whose integrity, and where necessary confidentiality, ordinary compromised software cannot defeat under the stated model |
| Trusted computing base | The components and authorities whose correct behavior is required for a particular claimed property |
| Explicit transfer | Delivery of modeled data through a named interface; it does not include every timing or physical observation |

ASKA 2.0 names internal fabrics, external channels, trust services and gateways by function.
